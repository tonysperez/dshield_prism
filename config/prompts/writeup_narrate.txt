Write 3 to 4 short paragraphs. NO markdown headers. NO bullet lists.
NO tables. NO code blocks. NO emojis. NO numbered sections.

Structure (as natural prose flow, not as labelled sections):

  Paragraph 1 — What happened.
    Open with the timeframe_phrase and scale_phrase together. Then
    state what_they_did in plain, observable terms (what an analyst
    watching the session would see). Do not include any judgment about
    intent or actor model yet.

  Paragraph 2 — What it is.
    State what_they_wanted as your interpretation. State the
    actor_model as your read of the data. Weave in the concerning_signals
    as the REASONS your interpretation is what it is — not as a list,
    as the sinews of the argument. If viability_assessment adds a useful
    signal (e.g. "the commands are valid but assume the attacker
    already has root, so this only matters post-login"), include it.

  Paragraph 3 — Confidence and gaps.
    Open with the confidence_band as your verdict, then explain it
    using confidence_reasoning. Surface the evidence_gaps as the things
    that would CHANGE your verdict — what an analyst would need to see
    to push from moderate to high, or to downgrade.

  Paragraph 4 — Defender takeaway. (OPTIONAL — only if defensive_angle
    is non-trivial.)
    One short paragraph naming the specific defender action the
    evidence implies. If defensive_angle is the generic "standard SSH
    hygiene" line, OMIT this paragraph entirely.

Citation rules — strict:
  - You may quote any "value" string from key_identifiers exactly as
    given, OR shortened by truncation only (e.g. sha256:c2c1... is
    fine, sha256:c2c1e9557c5abc30... is fine, but never invent a new
    hash). Always include a "label" alongside the value the first time
    you cite it: "the same RSA key (sha256:c2c1...)".
  - You MAY mention country codes, ASN numbers, sha256 prefixes, URLs,
    or IP addresses if and only if they appear in key_identifiers above.
  - You MAY mention high-level concepts that don't require concrete
    citation — "SSH brute force", "crypto miner", "credential spray",
    "Mirai-like behaviour" — when they describe a CATEGORY of activity
    and not a specific named indicator.
  - You MAY NOT introduce: a sha256 not in key_identifiers, an IP not
    in key_identifiers, a URL not in key_identifiers, a MITRE ID not
    in key_identifiers, an ASN not in key_identifiers, a country code
    not in key_identifiers, a CVE number under any circumstance unless
    it appears in key_identifiers.

Style rules — strict:
  - Active voice. Direct sentences. No hedging filler like
    "it's worth noting that", "in conclusion", "interestingly",
    "of course".
  - Confidence-hedged language ("appears", "consistent with",
    "suggests", "looks like") when interpreting; direct language
    ("installed", "ran", "appeared", "executed") when stating fact
    drawn from the brief.
  - Never editorialise: "this is a serious threat", "this is a
    sophisticated attack", "concerning activity". Show the reader the
    activity; let them conclude.
  - No emojis. No exclamation marks.

Output: the prose only. Nothing before, nothing after. No JSON
wrapper. No "Here is the writeup:" preamble.
